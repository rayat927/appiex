## 2. The funnel operating model

```
Relevant search, business community, partnerships, practical content
→ Intent-matched Web / AI / SEO service or portfolio page
→ Proof, deliverable clarity, useful resource, no hype
→ Free conversion calculator or technical checklist
→ Start Project (4-step buyer qualification)
→ Visitor-initiated WhatsApp or email handoff
→ Confirmed inbound message → human qualifying questions
→ Scoped proposal → accepted project → revenue
```

`/website-lead-calculator` is a **free, no-signup resource**: visitors provide traffic and rate assumptions, read the underlying math, and may copy their calculation. It is not a forecast, not a substitute for sales tracking, and the visitor's figures are not transmitted to Appiex servers.

`/start-project` is intentionally noindex, because it is a transactional form—not a keyword doorway page.

**Safety / delivery note:** A click to WhatsApp or email is intent, **not** a confirmed lead. The outstanding admin/order security PR should be independently validated before any sensitive, persistent CRM automation is released.

## 5. Ready-to-adapt LinkedIn post

**Hook:** 1,000 website visitors can mean 10 leads—or 20. But neither number tells you whether those leads can buy.

**Body:** A website can look beautiful and still attract the wrong audience. Start with the real funnel: visits → enquiries → qualified leads → closed clients. If 1,000 visits convert at 1%, that's 10 enquiries; at 2%, that's 20. Whether those extra enquiries are valuable depends on qualification, closing and your offer. That's why improving relevant clicks and conversion together matters more than maximizing traffic alone.

We published a simple, no-signup calculator so you can explore the assumptions yourself: https://appiex.com/website-lead-calculator

**Question:** What do you currently measure after someone clicks your website?

Always disclose your connection to Appiex when relevant; do not claim the example is a customer result.

An SME owner asked how to tell whether SEO helps the business. I would first separate Google impressions, actual visitors, enquiries, qualified prospects and real customers. ## 7. Reddit participation example (link-free by default)

**Question:** Is more website traffic always worth it for a small agency?

**Answer:** Not necessarily. I'd measure relevant enquiries and closed projects first. Suppose 1,000 sessions create 10 enquiries; if only 20% are qualified, that's 2 real opportunities. Doubling traffic from an unrelated audience may do little. I'd prioritize search intent, clearer service pages, examples from work I can substantiate, and low-friction contact. I also track outbound WhatsApp clicks separately from enquiries actually received, because a click isn't proof a message was sent.

Contribute as an actual professional. Disclose affiliation if relevant. Never mass-repost, use burner accounts, manipulate votes or bypass community-specific promotion restrictions.

## 9. Measurement implementation: every week

1. Google Search Console: clicks, impressions and CTR by *page and query*, with branded/nonbranded context verified; date-align comparisons.
2. GA4/GTM: `appiex_funnel_view`, `appiex_funnel_step_complete`, `appiex_funnel_prepared`, `appiex_funnel_contact_handoff`; confirm these fire once and never include personal details.
3. Calculator: page views, link clicks to `/start-project`, optional tool interactions; do not log user-entered numbers.
4. CRM or a privacy-safe log: confirmed enquiries, qualified enquiries, proposals sent, wins and invoiced revenue (only with suitable access controls).
5. Channel attribution: `utm_source`, `utm_medium=organic_social`, `utm_campaign`; distinguish referrals from paid acquisition.
6. Feedback loop: if CTR rises but enquiries do not, investigate **audience and content match**. If enquiries rise but deals do not, investigate **qualification and offer**.

**Success is not "100% of visitors convert".** Aim for appropriate visitors, truthful positioning, strong experience, confirmed enquiries, and profitable client relationships.
